IN PRACTICE

Ultrasonic tools need a liquid medium to transmit sound into the pipe wall.

In liquid lines, the product is the couplant. In gas lines, it is not available.

This is a physical constraint — not a technology gap.

ACME Integrity
GAS-APPLICABLE TECHNOLOGIES

Three technologies. Each answers a different integrity question.

MFL

Metal loss — corrosion, pitting, general wall thinning. All media. 2" to 56".

TFI

Longitudinal features — axial anomalies and seam weld defects that axial magnetization does not address.

EMAT

Cracking, SCC, and coating integrity — operates where ultrasonic contact tools cannot.

THE INSIGHT

In gas pipelines, there is less overlap between available technologies.

Each one covers a defined threat category. Missing a technology means missing a threat — not just reducing coverage.

The margin for imprecise tool selection is smaller in gas than in liquid service.

ACME Integrity INSPECTION APPROACH

The threat defines the technology. The strategy defines the run.

Single threat — one focused ACME Integrity technology run, matched to the pipeline's integrity profile.

Multiple threats — ACME Integrity combo runs combining MFL, TFI, or EMAT in a single inspection. One mobilization, multiple integrity answers.

Changed service conditions — re-evaluation of the inspection strategy to align with the current threat landscape.

Inspection planning, tool selection, execution, and analysis — delivered as a complete integrity program.
