IN PRACTICE

Repurposing existing gas pipelines for hydrogen transport is a practical path to decarbonization.

But hydrogen introduces new integrity concerns. Embrittlement. Accelerated fatigue. Material compatibility.

The pipeline that was safe for natural gas may not be safe for hydrogen without assessment.

2 / 6
ACME Integrity
THE CHALLENGE

Hydrogen affects the steel differently than natural gas.

Hydrogen embrittlement

Loss of ductility and fracture resistance, particularly at higher strength grades.

Accelerated fatigue crack growth

Existing sub-critical features may propagate faster under hydrogen service.

Material threshold

API 5L X52 and lower grades are generally more compatible. Higher grades require evaluation.

THE INSIGHT

A baseline inspection before conversion is not optional — it is the foundation of the conversion decision.

Every existing feature — crack, metal loss, seam weld anomaly — must be reassessed against hydrogen service conditions.

What was acceptable in natural gas may not be acceptable in hydrogen.

ACME Integrity — HYDROGEN PIPELINE INSPECTION

ACME Integrity is actively developing inspection strategies for hydrogen pipeline readiness assessment.

EMAT — baseline crack inspection in gas pipelines prior to conversion. No couplant. Coating disbondment mapping.

MFL + TFI — comprehensive metal loss and longitudinal feature assessment of existing condition.

PPIM 2026 — ACME Integrity presented with Saudi Aramco on non-destructive technologies for hydrogen pipelines.

Inspection planning for pipelines transitioning to hydrogen service.
