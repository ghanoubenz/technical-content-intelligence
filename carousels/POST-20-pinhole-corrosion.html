IN PRACTICE

A pinhole is a localized penetration — small in diameter, potentially deep in wall loss.

Detection depends on sensor resolution. A tool with coarse spacing may size the area but miss the localized depth.

Small features require high resolution. The specification determines what the inspection can find.

THE CHALLENGE

Why pinholes are difficult to detect and size.

Small footprint

A few millimeters in diameter — smaller than many sensor spacings.

Deep but narrow

High depth-to-width ratio. Coarse sensors average the depth over a wider area — undersizing the feature.

Critical consequence

A pinhole at 80% wall loss is a near-penetration — regardless of its small surface area.

THE INSIGHT

The sensor spacing determines the smallest feature the tool can reliably detect.

A 3mm pinhole cannot be reliably characterized by a sensor with 12mm spacing. The feature falls between sensors.

Resolution is not a specification detail. It is the difference between detection and missed threat.
