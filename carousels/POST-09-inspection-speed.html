IN PRACTICE

ILI tools operate within a specific velocity range.

Too fast — sensors cannot sample at the required resolution. Features may be undersized or missed. Too slow — tools may stall, creating gaps in coverage.

Pipeline conditions do not always produce ideal velocities.

THE FACTORS

What affects tool velocity in the pipeline.

Low-flow lines

Insufficient driving force to maintain target speed.

Elevation changes

Gravity-driven acceleration on downhill sections, deceleration on uphill.

Product slugging

Gas-liquid interfaces creating sudden speed changes.

THE INSIGHT

Data quality is determined by velocity consistency — not just velocity range.

A tool that alternates between stalling and racing produces data with gaps and resolution inconsistencies. Maintaining steady velocity within the target range is a measurement requirement.

Velocity control is not a convenience feature. It is a data quality requirement.
