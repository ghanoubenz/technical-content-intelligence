IN PRACTICE

Pipelines carrying H₂S-containing products face a different threat landscape.

Sour service accelerates hydrogen-induced damage — mechanisms distinct from standard corrosion or SCC.

The inspection technology must be compatible with the product. The assessment must account for hydrogen-related damage.

THE THREATS

Sour service produces specific damage mechanisms.

HIC — Hydrogen Induced Cracking

Internal blistering and stepwise cracking driven by hydrogen diffusion into the steel.

SOHIC — Stress-Oriented HIC

HIC stacking in the through-wall direction under residual or applied stress.

SSC — Sulfide Stress Cracking

Brittle fracture under tensile stress in H₂S environments — particularly in higher-hardness steels.

THE INSIGHT

Sour service damage looks different from standard corrosion and SCC.

HIC produces internal laminations and blisters. SOHIC creates through-wall stacking. SSC causes sudden brittle failure. Each requires specific detection capability.

The product defines the threat. The threat defines the required inspection technology.
