THE PROBLEM

MFL applies a magnetic field along the pipe axis.

This axial field is excellent at detecting circumferential features — corrosion, pitting, general metal loss.

But defects that run in the same direction as the magnetic field?

They don't disturb the flux. They stay hidden.

Axial grooving. Tunnel corrosion. Longitudinal seam weld anomalies. Narrow axial external corrosion.

These can be missed by standard MFL — regardless of resolution.

THE REALITY

It's not a quality issue. It's physics.

Magnetic flux leakage works by detecting disruptions in the magnetic field. If a defect is aligned with the field direction, the disruption is minimal — even if the defect is significant.

Analogy:

Imagine shining a flashlight across a wall to find cracks by their shadows. Cracks perpendicular to the light cast shadows. Cracks parallel to the light? Invisible.

Standard MFL's axial field catches circumferential defects well. For longitudinal defects, you need a different field orientation.

THE INSIGHT

This is why TFI exists.

Transverse Field Inspection applies the magnetic field in the circumferential direction — perpendicular to the pipe axis.

What TFI catches that standard MFL doesn't:

Same physics principle. Different field orientation. Different threats found.
