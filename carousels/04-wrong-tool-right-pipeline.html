THE PROBLEM

An operator ran MFL on a pipeline with a known SCC history.

The MFL report showed no critical features.

No significant metal loss. No gouges. No immediate concerns.

The operator filed the report and moved on.

But the cracks were already there. MFL was never designed to find them.

THE REALITY

MFL and UTCD use different physics. They answer different questions.

MFL — Magnetic Flux Leakage

Saturates the pipe wall magnetically. Detects where wall material is missing. Finds: corrosion, pitting, gouging, general metal loss.

ACME Integrity MFL: 2"–56", depth accuracy 0.1t at 90% POD.

UTCD — Ultrasonic Crack Detection

Sends ultrasonic pulses into the wall. Detects reflections from crack-like features. Finds: SCC, fatigue cracks, seam weld anomalies, hook cracks.

Different physics. Different purpose. Not interchangeable.

THE INSIGHT

The failure wasn't in the tool. It was in the selection.

A clean MFL report on a pipeline with crack threats doesn't mean the pipeline is safe.

It means you answered the wrong question.

MFL asks: "How much wall is left?"

UTCD asks: "Are there cracks growing?"

TFI asks: "Are there longitudinal anomalies MFL can't see?"

If your threat assessment doesn't drive your tool selection, your inspection data is incomplete.

THE DECISION FRAMEWORK

Start with the threat. Then select the tool.

Corrosion-dominant threat

MFL (2"–56") — metal loss, pitting, general thinning

Crack-dominant threat

UTCD — SCC, fatigue, seam weld, hook cracks

Longitudinal anomaly risk

TFI (4"–56") — axial grooving, tunnel corrosion, weld anomalies

Dry gas / no liquid couplant

EMAT — crack + wall loss detection without liquid medium

Multiple threats / HCA

Combined runs — MFL + UTCD, or MFL + TFI + Caliper

DECISION

A clean report from the wrong tool is more dangerous than no report at all.

Because it creates confidence where there should be questions.
