## CAROUSEL 06: "Coating disbondment is not a defect. It is a future defect location."

### LinkedIn Caption:

Coating disbondment is often treated as a secondary finding in ILI reports.

In practice, it is one of the most valuable.

When coating separates, the pipe loses its primary barrier. CP shielding begins. Moisture and near-neutral pH conditions reach the steel directly.

These are the documented precursors to SCC initiation.

A disbondment identified today may show no active cracking — but it marks where cracking is most likely to develop next.

ACME Integrity EMAT inspections detect both cracks and coating disbondment in a single run — in gas and liquid pipelines. 120% overlapping surface coverage. Disbondment locations are mapped and prioritized for monitoring or direct assessment as part of the inspection program.

Knowing where a threat exists is necessary.
Knowing where it will develop next is proactive integrity.

## CAROUSEL 07: "Gas pipelines have fewer inspection options."

### LinkedIn Caption:

Ultrasonic contact-based tools need a liquid medium. In gas pipelines, that medium is not available.

This is a physical constraint — not a technology gap.

Three technologies remain applicable: MFL for metal loss, TFI for longitudinal and seam weld features, EMAT for cracking, SCC, and coating integrity.

In liquid lines, there is significant overlap between technologies. In gas, there is less. Each one covers a defined threat category. Missing a technology means missing a threat.

ACME Integrity designs gas pipeline inspection strategies around the threat profile — matching MFL, TFI, and EMAT to the specific integrity questions. Combo runs address multiple threats in a single mobilization. Inspection planning, tool selection, execution, and analysis delivered as a complete program.

Fewer options does not mean simpler decisions. It means each one matters more.
