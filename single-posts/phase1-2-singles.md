## POST 08 — Seam weld integrity is a separate question.
**Phase:** 1 | **Target:** Weld-focused teams | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

The pipe body and the longitudinal seam weld are manufactured differently, inspected differently, and fail differently.

Seam welds can contain manufacturing anomalies — lack of fusion, hook cracks, hard spots — that remain dormant for decades before becoming active under fatigue or pressure cycling.

Inspecting the pipe body for corrosion does not address seam weld integrity. These are separate integrity questions.

TFI circumferential magnetization addresses longitudinal features that axial MFL does not. EMAT detects seam weld cracking in gas pipelines. ACME Integrity inspection strategies for seam weld assessment use TFI and EMAT — selected based on product type, threat profile, and weld history.

## POST 03 — Corrosion growth between inspections.
**Phase:** 2 | **Target:** Asset managers | **CTA:** Soft (website)

### LinkedIn Caption:

A pipeline inspected five years ago showed metal loss at 30% wall thickness.

Today, that feature may be at 50% — or more. Growth rate depends on the environment, the product, and operating conditions. It is rarely linear.

Re-inspection intervals based on assumed growth rates carry risk. Intervals based on measured growth — comparing successive ILI data sets — carry data.

ACME Integrity inspection programs include corrosion growth assessment using successive runs. Features are tracked individually. Growth rates calculated per feature, not assumed globally.

The integrity assessment is only as reliable as the growth model behind it.

## POST 09 — Inspection speed affects data quality.
**Phase:** 2 | **Target:** Operations + inspection teams | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

ILI tools operate within a specific velocity range.

Too fast — sensors cannot sample at the required resolution. Features may be undersized or missed. Too slow — tools may stall, creating gaps in coverage.

Pipeline conditions do not always produce ideal velocities. Low-flow lines, elevation changes, product slugging — all affect tool speed.

ACME Integrity ILI tools include active speed control to maintain consistent velocity within the target range. EMAT: 0.3–2.0 m/s. UTWM: 0.3–2.0 m/s standard, 4.0 optional. MFL: 0.2–5.0 m/s.

Data quality depends on velocity control. Velocity control depends on tool engineering.

## POST 06 — Not every anomaly is a threat.
**Phase:** 2 | **Target:** Integrity engineers | **CTA:** Soft

### LinkedIn Caption:

An ILI report may contain hundreds of reported features.

Not all require action. The difference between a reported feature and an actionable threat is the integrity assessment.

Depth, length, orientation, interaction, proximity to welds, operating pressure, material grade — all determine whether a feature is deferred, monitored, or repaired.

Raw data identifies features. Engineering assessment determines their significance.

ACME Integrity integrity services include fitness-for-purpose assessment, feature interaction analysis, and repair prioritization — turning inspection data into decisions.

## POST 14 — Geometry inspection is more than dent detection.
**Phase:** 2 | **Target:** Integrity + geotech teams | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

Geometry inspection is often associated with dent detection.

In practice, it addresses a broader set of integrity questions: ovality, buckles, wrinkles, bend strain, and pipeline movement over time.

In areas with geotechnical activity, thermal cycling, or seismic risk, geometry data reveals whether the pipeline is experiencing strain — not just whether it has a dent.

ACME Integrity Smart AI Caliper delivers automated assessment of geometry features, girth welds, bends, and stress-strain zones. Final reports on-site in 9 hours maximum. 3D pipeline visualization. Detection of small off-takes including illegal tapping.

The shape of the pipe tells a story about the forces acting on it.
