**File:** carousels/06-coating-disbondment-future-threat.html
**Phase:** 1 | **Target:** Integrity teams | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

Coating disbondment is not a defect today.

It is the location of a future defect.

When coating separates from the pipe surface, it creates a shielded environment — moisture, oxygen, and chemistry trapped against bare steel. The conditions for corrosion or stress corrosion cracking develop beneath the surface, invisible to external survey.

Disbondment does not damage the pipe. It enables the mechanisms that will.

ACME Integrity EMAT technology detects coating disbondment during in-line inspection — without requiring couplant. In gas pipelines, it maps disbonded zones alongside crack detection in a single run.

What you cannot see from the outside may already be developing underneath.

## POST 09 (S) — Inspection speed affects data quality.
**Phase:** 2 | **Target:** Operations + inspection teams | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

ILI tools operate within a specific velocity range.

Too fast — sensors cannot sample at the required resolution. Features may be undersized or missed. Too slow — tools may stall, creating gaps in coverage.

Pipeline conditions do not always produce ideal velocities. Low-flow lines, elevation changes, product slugging — all affect tool speed.

ACME Integrity ILI tools include active speed control to maintain consistent velocity within the target range. EMAT: 0.3–2.0 m/s. UTWM: 0.3–2.0 m/s standard, 4.0 optional. MFL: 0.2–5.0 m/s.

Data quality depends on velocity control. Velocity control depends on tool engineering.

## POST 32 (S) — Internal cracking is a different problem than external cracking.
**Phase:** New Tools | **Target:** Integrity engineers | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

Most crack detection ILI tools are designed to find cracks initiating from the external surface — SCC, fatigue from environmental exposure, coating-related mechanisms.

Internal surface cracking is a different failure mode. It initiates from the inside — driven by corrosion fatigue, hydrogen-induced damage, or operational stress at the internal wall.

The detection approach must account for the crack origin direction. A tool optimized for external cracks may not detect internal surface features with the same sensitivity.

ACME Integrity's Internal Crack Survey (ICS) technology is specifically designed for cracks initiating from the internal pipe surface. Applicable in gas and liquid pipelines, wall thickness 4–13mm.

As pipelines are evaluated for hydrogen service, internal cracking from hydrogen damage becomes a primary concern. The inspection must be designed for the specific mechanism — not assumed to be covered by standard crack detection.

## POST 33 (C) — Tri-Axial MFL sees what standard MFL averages out.

**Phase:** New Tools | **Target:** Inspection engineers | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

Standard MFL uses axial magnetization — effective for general corrosion and pitting. But complex-shaped features with both axial and circumferential extent may be averaged out by a single measurement axis.

Tri-Axial MFL measures in three independent axes (X, Y, Z) simultaneously — capturing groove-like features, selective seam corrosion, and irregular metal loss patterns that single-axis measurement cannot fully characterize.

ACME Integrity Tri-Axial MFL: 3mm circumferential sensor spacing, 1.5mm axial sampling, wall range 3–25.4mm. The highest resolution available for complex feature mapping.

More measurement axes means less ambiguity in feature characterization.

## POST 34 (S) — MTM inspects pipelines without entering them.
**Phase:** New Tools | **Target:** Unpiggable operators | **CTA:** None

### LinkedIn Caption:

Some pipelines cannot accommodate any internal tool — no access, no traps, no bore clearance, no shutdown window.

Magnetic Tomography Method (MTM) inspects the pipeline from the outside. A walk-along survey measures distortions in the earth's magnetic field caused by stress concentration, developing corrosion, and fatigue damage in the pipe wall.

No pipeline entry. No product disruption. No shutdown required.

The survey covers up to 10 km per day. Detected anomalies are classified into three risk categories — immediate action, scheduled assessment, or monitoring. Results are GPS-referenced for precise location.

ACME Integrity MTM surveys are deployed on underground and offshore pipelines from 100mm to 1400mm diameter — providing integrity data for lines that were previously considered uninspectable.
