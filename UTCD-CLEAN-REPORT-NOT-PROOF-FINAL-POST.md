## Caption

A clean ILI report is not proof of integrity.

It is proof of what that inspection was designed to measure.

That difference matters.

Every inspection technology has a detection boundary. Every report answers a specific question within that boundary.

The risk begins when the report is treated as if it answered a different question.

If cracking is a credible threat, the inspection plan has to account for crack geometry, crack orientation, and the physics needed to detect planar flaws.

This week, ACME Integrity completed assembly of a 47" UTCD tool for deployment in KSA.

The point is not the tool alone.

The point is the inspection question:

Did the last campaign prove the threat is absent?

Or did it only prove that the selected method did not report it?

Detection limits are not footnotes.

They are part of the integrity decision.
