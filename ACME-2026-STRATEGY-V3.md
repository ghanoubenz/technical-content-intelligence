# ACME Integrity 2026 LinkedIn Strategy
## Built for Pipeline Operators, Not for Algorithms

---

## The Problem With Most ILI Company Content

It reads like a product brochure. It educates at a Wikipedia level. It could come from any competitor. It doesn't challenge, confront, or change how the audience thinks.

Pipeline operators and integrity engineers don't need another "What is MFL?" post. They need someone who understands their real problems and makes them think harder about their decisions.

---

## Who We're Talking To

| Role | What keeps them up at night |
|------|----------------------------|
| Integrity Engineer | "Did I select the right tool? Will the data hold up in a regulatory review?" |
| Technical Manager | "Can I justify this spend? What if the inspection misses something?" |
| Pipeline Operator / Asset Owner | "What's my actual risk exposure? Am I over-inspecting or under-inspecting?" |
| Procurement / Planning | "How do I write a spec that actually gets me what I need?" |

They stop scrolling for:
1. Statements that challenge their current practice
2. Specific scenarios they recognize from last quarter
3. Decision frameworks they can save and reuse
4. Technical credibility they can trust

They keep scrolling past:
- "Did you know..." educational posts
- Generic infographics
- "We're proud to announce..."
- Emoji-heavy captions
- Content that sounds like a textbook summary

---

## The Content Thesis

Every post answers one question:

**"What does ACME Integrity know that others don't -- and why should I care?"**

Not: "Here's what MFL is."
But: "Here's what MFL misses -- and what that costs you."

Not: "We offer UTCD services."
But: "A clean MFL report does not mean your pipeline has no cracks."

## Caption Rules

1. First line = a statement that challenges an assumption. No warm-ups.
2. No emojis. Engineers don't respond to fire emojis and lightbulbs.
3. Short paragraphs. White space. Reads like an engineering memo, not a marketing post.
4. End with a hard question or a hard stop. Never "What do you think?"
5. Address specific roles: "If you're managing a 20-year-old crude line in a sour environment..."
6. No hashtag walls. Maximum 5-6 targeted hashtags in the first comment.

## CTA Philosophy

| Frequency | Type | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| 70% of posts | No CTA | Pure value. Trust accumulates. |
| 20% of posts | Soft CTA in first comment | "DM us if you want the full decision framework." |
| 10% of posts | Direct CTA | Webinar registration only. |

Never sell on a post that educates. The education IS the selling.

---

## What to Avoid

- "We're excited to share..."
- "Did you know that pipeline integrity..."
- Generic tech explainers anyone could write
- Stock imagery or 3D renders that look fake
- Carousel slides with more than 40 words
- CTAs on every post
- Celebrating company milestones nobody outside cares about
- Content that sounds like it was written by AI summarizing a textbook
- Posting just to fill a schedule
- Emoji-driven engagement bait
- Reposting industry news without a unique take
- "Proud to announce" language for routine operations

## Webinars (3 Per Year -- Only If They Earn Attention)

### 1. "Tool Selection for Mixed-Threat Pipelines" (June 2026)
- Format: 45 min. Screen share + camera. Zoom or Teams. No slides deck -- show real data.
- Content: ACME Integrity integrity engineer walks through 3 anonymized pipeline scenarios. Each one: "Here's the pipeline. Here's the threat register. Here's why we chose this tool combination."
- Why it works: Operators face this decision every quarter. Nobody teaches it.
- Promotion: LinkedIn Event 3 weeks before. Two teaser posts. One follow-up with key takeaway clips.
- Execution: Record. Cut into 3-5 short clips for LinkedIn. One clip per scenario.

### 2. "Reading Between the Lines of Your ILI Report" (September 2026)
- Format: 45 min. Senior data analyst walks through anonymized report sections.
- Content: What "confidence level" actually means. What "within detection limits" means. How to read feature tables. How to question sizing accuracy.
- Why it works: This is the most commercially valuable webinar. It builds trust by showing transparency. Every integrity engineer receives reports -- few know how to challenge them.
- Promotion: Same approach. Tease with "most operators read the summary page and skip to the feature list" type posts.

### 3. "2027 Inspection Campaign Planning: A Framework" (Late November 2026)
- Format: 30 min. Structured decision framework.
- Content: Step 1: Threat register review. Step 2: Technology gap analysis. Step 3: Timing optimization. Step 4: What to actually put in your ILI RFP.
- Why it works: Consultative. Not a sales pitch. The framework is the value. Operators are planning budgets for next year.
- Promotion: LinkedIn Event. Offer the planning framework as a PDF after.

### Webinar Execution Rules:
- No fancy production. A screen share + face is more credible than a polished studio.
- Maximum 45 minutes including Q&A.
- Always record. Always clip. One webinar = 4-6 LinkedIn posts.
- Never pitch ACME Integrity services during the webinar. Let the expertise speak.

## Visual Identity (GPT + Claude Prompts)

### GPT Image Prompt Standards:
- Always specify: camera angle, lighting type, surface material, environment context
- Prefer: "industrial photography" over "3D render"
- Include: "No text overlay. No people. No logos. Clean composition."
- Specify aspect ratio: 1080x1350 portrait for carousel covers, 1200x628 for single images
- Avoid: "dark background" as a lazy default. Specify the actual environment.

### Claude Design Prompt Standards:
- Always specify: layout structure, typography hierarchy, color treatment, data visualization style, overall mood
- Reference: "Engineering report aesthetic" not "marketing brochure"
- Include: specific slide-by-slide layout direction for carousels
- Mood keywords: clinical, authoritative, restrained, data-rich, precise
- Avoid: "modern and clean" (too vague), "professional" (meaningless)

## Measurement (What Actually Matters)

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Save rate | >3% per post | Engineers save useful content. This is the #1 signal of value. |
| Comment quality | Technical replies, not "Great post!" | Real engagement = real authority. |
| DM inquiries | 2-4 per month | The actual commercial signal. |
| Follower growth | 150-250/month | Steady compound growth from quality, not virality. |
| Webinar registrations | 80-120 per event | Targeted > massive. |

Vanity metrics to ignore: Impressions (unless trending), likes without comments, follower count without engagement rate.
