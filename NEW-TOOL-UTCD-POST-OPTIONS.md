## OPTION 2 — "H₂S Doesn't Just Corrode. It Cracks."
**Angle:** Sour service damage — HIC, SOHIC, SSC — and why MFL alone misses it.
**Carousel file:** `carousels/NEW-TOOL-OPTION-2-sour-service-cracking.html`
**Best for:** Targeting Middle East / KSA audience directly. Sour service is the baseline in that region, so this post speaks to the exact operators you want the tool to reach. Likely highest engagement from Saudi Aramco, ADNOC, and similar operators.

### LinkedIn Caption

In sour service, H₂S is not just a corrosion problem. It is a cracking problem.

Atomic hydrogen dissociates at the pipe wall and diffuses into the steel lattice. It accumulates at inclusions and grain boundaries. The result is damage that does not show on the external surface:

HIC — Hydrogen-Induced Cracking. Internal laminations running parallel to the pipe wall. Invisible from outside.

SOHIC — Stress-Oriented HIC. Stacked laminations that connect through the wall thickness under stress.

SSC — Sulfide Stress Cracking. Sharp, planar cracks in high-hardness regions — HAZ, welds, cold-worked areas.

MFL was designed to detect volumetric metal loss. It is not optimized for planar cracks or mid-wall laminations. In sour service, the most dangerous threats are exactly the ones MFL was not built to find.

We just finished assembly of a new UTCD tool in our facility. It is calibrated, tested, and shipping to KSA this week — heading to a region where sour service is not an exception, it is the baseline.

If your pipeline carries sour product, the question is not whether you are running ILI. It is whether the ILI you are running can actually find HIC.

**Carousel file:** `carousels/NEW-TOOL-OPTION-3-silent-threats.html`

### LinkedIn Caption

The most dangerous pipeline defects are the quiet ones.

Stress corrosion cracking develops in silence. Under disbonded coating, moisture reaches bare steel. With the right chemistry and tensile stress, fine cracks begin to grow — branched, tight, nearly invisible.

No external bulge. No pressure drop. No warning. Until rupture.

MFL was built for metal loss, not planar cracks. A crack removes almost no volume, so the flux leakage signal is weak or absent. Axial MFL is least sensitive to the exact orientation SCC prefers. When a crack signal does appear, it is hard to distinguish from benign features.

This is not a criticism of MFL. It is a statement about what it was designed to do.

Crack detection is not an upgrade to metal loss inspection. It is a different question — and it needs a different tool.
