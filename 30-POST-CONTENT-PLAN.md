## POST 03 (S) — Single Post: Corrosion growth between inspections

### LinkedIn Caption:

A pipeline inspected five years ago showed metal loss at 30% wall thickness.

Today, that same feature may be at 50% — or more. The growth rate depends on the environment, the product, and the operating conditions. It is rarely linear.

Re-inspection intervals based on assumed growth rates carry risk. Intervals based on measured growth — comparing two or more ILI data sets — carry data.

ACME Integrity inspection programs include corrosion growth assessment using successive ILI runs. Features are tracked individually. Growth rates are calculated per feature, not assumed globally.

The integrity assessment is only as reliable as the growth model behind it.

The pipe body and the longitudinal seam weld are manufactured differently, inspected differently, and fail differently.

Seam welds can contain manufacturing anomalies — lack of fusion, hook cracks, hard spots — that may remain dormant for decades before becoming active under fatigue or pressure cycling.

Inspecting the pipe body for corrosion does not address seam weld integrity. These are separate integrity questions requiring separate technology approaches.

TFI circumferential magnetization addresses longitudinal features that axial MFL does not. EMAT detects seam weld cracking in gas pipelines where ultrasonic tools cannot operate.

ACME Integrity inspection strategies for seam weld assessment use TFI and EMAT — selected based on product type, threat profile, and weld history.

## POST 09 (S) — Single Post: Inspection speed affects data quality.

### LinkedIn Caption:

ILI tools are designed to operate within a specific velocity range.

Too fast — sensors cannot sample at the required resolution. Data quality drops. Features may be undersized or missed entirely.

In practice, pipeline operating conditions do not always produce ideal tool velocities. Low-flow lines, steep elevation changes, and product slugging all affect tool speed.

## POST 32 (S) — Single Post: Internal cracking is a different problem than external cracking.

### LinkedIn Caption:

Most crack detection ILI tools are designed to find cracks initiating from the external surface — SCC, fatigue from environmental exposure, coating-related mechanisms.

Internal surface cracking is a different failure mode. It initiates from the inside — driven by corrosion fatigue, hydrogen-induced damage, or operational stress at the internal wall.

The detection approach must account for the crack origin direction. A tool optimized for external cracks may not detect internal surface features with the same sensitivity.

ACME Integrity's Internal Crack Survey (ICS) technology is specifically designed for cracks initiating from the internal pipe surface. Applicable in gas and liquid pipelines, wall thickness 4–13mm.

As pipelines are evaluated for hydrogen service, internal cracking from hydrogen damage becomes a primary concern. The inspection must be designed for the specific mechanism — not assumed to be covered by standard crack detection.

## POST 33 (C) — Tri-Axial MFL sees what standard MFL averages out.

### Slide 1 — Hook
**Tag:** INSPECTION TECHNOLOGY
**Headline:** Standard MFL measures in one axis.
**Sub:** Complex metal loss exists in three.

### Slide 2 — In Practice
**Tag:** IN PRACTICE
**Headline:** Standard MFL uses axial magnetization. It measures flux leakage along one primary direction.
**Body:** For general corrosion and pitting, this is effective. For complex-shaped features — where metal loss has both axial and circumferential extent — a single axis may average out the true geometry.

### Slide 3 — The Difference
**Tag:** THE TECHNOLOGY
**Headline:** Tri-Axial MFL measures magnetic flux leakage in three independent axes: X, Y, and Z.
**Cards:**
- **Axial component** — detects circumferentially oriented features.
- **Radial component** — measures depth and volume of metal loss.
- **Circumferential component** — detects axially oriented features and complex shapes.
**Key line:** Three measurements. One feature. A more complete characterization.

### Slide 4 — The Insight
**Tag:** THE INSIGHT
**Headline:** Complex metal loss features are not always simple pits or uniform wall thinning.
**Body:** Selective seam corrosion, groove-like features, and irregular metal loss patterns are better characterized when measured from multiple magnetic axes simultaneously.
**Key line:** More measurement axes means less ambiguity in feature characterization.

### Slide 5 — ACME Integrity Capability
