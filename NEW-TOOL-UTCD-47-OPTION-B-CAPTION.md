## CAPTION

A clean ILI report does not mean your pipeline has no cracks.

It means the tool you ran did not find any — within its own detection limits.

That distinction matters more than most operators realize.

MFL detects flux leakage from missing metal. Corrosion pits remove volume. The signal is clear. The sizing is reliable.

A crack removes no volume. It separates the steel along a plane. No material is missing. The flux passes through with little or no disruption. The signal is weak, ambiguous, or absent entirely.

The tool is working correctly. It was never designed for this.

So when an MFL report says "0 crack-type indications," it is not confirming the absence of cracks. It is confirming the absence of something MFL was built to detect. Those are two very different statements.

The risk is not the tool. The risk is treating the report as if it answered a question it was never asked.

This week we finished assembly of a 47" UTCD tool — ultrasonic crack detection, built to find the planar flaws that MFL physics cannot reach. It ships to KSA for deployment on a liquid pipeline where cracking is a credible threat.

Not because the last report found something. Because the last report could not.

A detection limit is not a footnote. It is the real answer your inspection gives.
